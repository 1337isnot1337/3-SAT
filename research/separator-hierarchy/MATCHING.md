# Why low-occurrence expander formulas can be easy

Status: standard known argument, reproduced here as a limitation of the preceding obstruction; substantially AI-assisted, without independent review. The bounded-occurrence satisfiability threshold is due to Craig A. Tovey, *A Simplified NP-Complete Satisfiability Problem*, Discrete Applied Mathematics 8 (1984), https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(84)90081-7. The author's [publication list](https://sites.gatech.edu/craig-tovey/publications/) states the threshold and the no-repeated-variable condition; its HTML fetched 2026-10-28 had SHA-256 `b4a84c366a17ff948ee45c7711d75b01a64f1948f915bbe207c2080cfdaad6aa`. No novelty is claimed.

Let a CNF have at least r distinct variables in every clause and let each variable occur in at most r clauses, for a positive integer r. Treat clauses as distinct even when identical. Form the bipartite incidence graph from clauses to variables. For every set C of clauses, there are at least r|C| incidence edges, while at most r|N(C)| meet its neighboring variables. Thus |N(C)|>=|C|. Hall's theorem gives a matching covering all clauses. Assign each matched variable to satisfy the literal it matches; because matched variables are distinct, every clause is satisfied. A standard bipartite matching algorithm constructs this satisfying assignment in polynomial time.

In particular, exact-width-three clauses on distinct variables with at most three clause occurrences per variable are always satisfiable, even if the primal graph is an expander and even without the explicit planted assignment in LINE-GRAPH.md. The two-occurrence construction there cannot witness a difficult decision case. The broader class of 3-CNF allows clauses with fewer than three distinct variables, and neither this argument nor that conclusion applies to it. For example, the five clauses

    (a OR b), (a OR NOT b), (NOT c OR d), (NOT c OR NOT d), (NOT a OR c)

are unsatisfiable: the first two force a, the next two force NOT c, and the last forbids that combination. Each variable occurs at most three times, each appears in both polarities, and there are no units. This example also illustrates that short clauses arising after restrictions must be charged, even if the original formula had only width-three clauses.

A decision-only implementation of the separator recursion can invoke polynomial-time matching on a residual child and return YES for that child whenever a clause-covering matching is found. This certificate is sufficient, not necessary; failed matching does not imply unsatisfiability. It does not compute the exact counts used by the counting wrapper, and it gives no improved worst-case guarantee for arbitrary inputs. One may use matching opportunistically and fall back to the existing decision recursion; no claimed bound depends on its success.

`python3 -I -B research/separator-hierarchy/matching.py` constructs and verifies matching assignments for 1,200 seeded bounded-occurrence exact-width-three formulas and checks the four-variable unsatisfiable short-clause example exhaustively. These are finite controls, not a proof of Hall's theorem or of the all-size statement.
