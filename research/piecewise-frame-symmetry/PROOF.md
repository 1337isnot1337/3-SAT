# Two Pauli label frames generate a large symmetry group

This is an author-checked, substantially AI-assisted argument; it has not
received independent or formal review. The predecessor's unfused F8
classes and Fourier-support crossing model are in
[three-bit-macro-walsh](../three-bit-macro-walsh/README.md). The separate
unfused entropy argument uses a common type-symmetric frame; this package
studies a changed, piece-dependent frame premise.

## Statement

For R>=2 macros write each Pauli label as `(a,b)` with
`a,b in F_2^(3R)` and `Q(a,b)=a dot b`. In each three-bit block identify
the a coordinate with `A in F_8` and the dual b coordinate with
`B in F_8`. The ten local classes are zero, the two nonzero axes, and
`AB=t` for each nonzero `t in F_8`. Let G be generated by independent
local maps `(A,B)->(uA,u^-1 B)` and macro permutations. Its orbits are
exactly the global ten-class types.

Let L be the quadratic-isometric CNOT `a_(2,0) += a_(1,0)` and
`b_(1,0) += b_(2,0)`, on one bit from each of two macros. The group
generated by `G` and `L G L^-1` is precisely

```math
 { (a,b) -> (M a,M^{-T} b) : M in GL(3R,2) }.
```

It has five orbits: zero; the two nonzero coordinate axes; and the
nonzero pairs with `Q=0` or `Q=1`. Every nonzero function invariant under
both frame symmetry groups has a nonzero value on an original ten-class
type containing at most `R*7^R` words. More strongly, the minimum type
entropy among its support is at most `log2(14)` per macro.

Thus a single dual that must be type-constant in both frames cannot meet
the entropy-floor condition `F>3 sqrt(2)` used by the preceding
single-seed certificate method. This does not produce crossing pieces,
disprove another certificate method, or improve a SAT bound. It explains
why allowing different pieces to use different frames is a genuinely
changed premise relative to a *common* relabeling.

## Generate GL from the two type symmetries

G contains independent multiplication by each nonzero F8 scalar on the
a blocks and the corresponding inverse-transpose action on the b blocks.
L induces on the a side the transvection `I+E`, where E maps one bit of
block 1 to one bit of block 2. For any nontrivial local scaling S of block
2, both `L S L^-1` and `S^-1` lie in the generated group. Because E maps
between distinct blocks and `E S E=0`, their product on the a side is

```math
 (I+E) S (I+E) S^-1 = I + E + S E S^-1.
```

This is a cross-block rank-one transvection: it applies the same nonzero
source functional as E and has nonzero target vector `v+Sv`. Independent
F8 scalings of the source and target act transitively on their nonzero
linear functionals and nonzero vectors, respectively. Conjugating this
one transvection therefore yields every rank-one map between those two
blocks. Macro permutations provide every ordered pair of distinct blocks.
In particular, all cross-block elementary matrices occur. For two distinct
bits i,j in the same block, choose a bit k in another block; the commutator
of elementary transvections `I+E_ik` and `I+E_kj` is `I+E_ij`. Thus all
elementary matrices on `3R` bits occur and generate `GL(3R,2)`.
Conversely every generator is of the displayed `M,M^-T` form, proving
equality. The [checker](verify.py) explicitly validates all 49 local
rank-one cross maps and the within-block commutators.

GL acts transitively on nonzero vectors, and its stabilizer is transitive
on nonzero covectors with a prescribed value on that vector. This proves
the five orbits. Their sizes are `1`, `2^n-1` twice,
`(2^n-1)(2^(n-1)-1)` and `(2^n-1)2^(n-1)`, where `n=3R`.
The checker enumerates the actual generated orbits for R=2,3 and verifies
all quadratic values, providing a finite control rather than replacing
the all-R generation proof.

## Small types in every orbit

The zero orbit contains the all-zero type. The two axis orbits contain
types in which every macro occupies the same nonzero axis class, each of
size `7^R`. For the `Q=0` mixed orbit choose every product equal to one
fixed nonzero trace-zero field element; this type also has size `7^R`.
For the `Q=1` mixed orbit choose one product of trace one and the other
R-1 equal to a fixed nonzero trace-zero element; that type has size
`R*7^R`. Such trace-zero and trace-one nonzero elements exist in F8.

The corresponding type entropies are at most `log2(7)+1=log2(14)` per
macro, since the binary entropy of `(1/R,1-1/R)` is at most one. As
`14<16` and `4<3 sqrt(2)`, no nonzero invariant dual can have all its
supporting types above the floor required for that entropy method. This
argument applies whether or not the invariant dual is self-Fourier; it
therefore also covers all of the common-dual seeds of that method.

## A bounded two-frame atom screen

For R=2, [two_frame_screen.py](two_frame_screen.py) constructs the
55-vector rational adapted basis of the ten-class type space, ordered by
actual type cardinality, using the predecessor's elimination algorithm.
Treat those basis vectors and their L-pullbacks as a fixed atom alphabet;
charge each atom by the sum of the ceiling base-two logs of its exact
original and Walsh support cardinalities. L preserves both supports.

At threshold 20 bits, the 48 qualifying atoms per frame have a 96-column
two-frame evaluation matrix. Its rank is 92 modulo 65,521, while adjoining
the target column gives rank 93. Four explicit independent *rational*
column dependencies bound its characteristic-zero rank by 92, so the
modular augmented rank excludes a rational representation. The one-frame
48-column matrix has full modular column rank, and its augmented rank is
49, yielding the same exclusion. At 21 bits all 55 original adapted-basis
vectors are available and span the target exactly over the rationals.
Thus the minimum maximum atom cost in this *fixed alphabet* is 21 bits
for both one and two frames. The checker compresses the 4,096 word rows
to 853 distinct pairs of original and transformed types; it verifies the
four exact dependencies on every pair.

The same exclusion also covers any number of pieces whose two supports
are *certified* by pairs of prefixes in the common type order, provided
the sum of the ceiling logs of the prefix cardinalities is at most 20.
Indeed the predecessor's adapted-basis construction simultaneously
adapts the two prefix flags: their intersection is spanned by those
adapted vectors whose input index and output pivot lie in the respective
prefixes. Each of these spanning vectors is itself among the checked
20-bit atoms. The argument applies separately in either frame.

This is a finite result for the specified atom and prefix-certified
alphabets, not a lower bound on arbitrary low-support linear combinations,
other bases, larger R, or general two-frame pieces. In particular, cancellations inside a different
atom can change its support and are not covered by this screen.

## Open construction

Try a polynomial-size decomposition whose pieces use either the original
frame or its L-conjugate while retaining two individually cheap Pauli
supports. The group result only blocks the *shared dual* proof route; it
says nothing about whether such a decomposition exists. Uniform support
indexing, factor entries, coefficient heights, both cut costs, recursion,
query encoding, precision and recovery remain unpaid. The selected
result and the accepted source are unchanged.
