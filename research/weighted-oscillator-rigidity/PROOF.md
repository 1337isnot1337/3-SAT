# Rigidity of scalar-weighted oscillator repairs

This is an author-checked necessary condition for a particular modification of
Anthropic's crossing construction, not a SAT lower bound. The source and its
precise h=1 convention are pinned in `research/sparse-oscillator-locality/PROOF.md`
(base main `630041c9da3a26edb8c8f59d5f8a4132a188c8bd`). The accepted OpenAI
baseline is assumed, not audited. We credit that predecessor for the
second-difference argument and ask whether *arbitrary scalar weights* repair
its missing-mode example.

Let F be a field of characteristic zero. On F[t_1,t_2,...], let
`D_-k = a_k t_k` and `D_k = b_k partial/partial t_k` for k>0, with
`D_0=0`; any a_k or b_k may vanish. Define g(0)=0 and
`g(k)=a_k b_k` for k>0, `g(-k)=-g(k)`. Then
`[D_k,D_-k]=g(k) Id`. Retain exactly the predecessor's h=1 two-order
identity, namely

    Q_s - Q_s^rev = -(g(s-1)-2g(s)+g(s+1)) Id.

This follows by commuting the three pairs of operators with coefficients
1, -2, 1; it holds on the whole polynomial ring, including the vacuum.
It follows that this h=1 identity holds at every integer s **if and only if**

    a_k b_k = c k    for all k>=1, with c=a_1 b_1.       (1)

Indeed zero second differences force `g(k)=g(0)+k(g(1)-g(0))`
for positive and negative k; conversely this linear g has zero second
differences. The characteristic-zero assumption matters below.

If c is nonzero, (1) forces both weights nonzero at every index: deletion
of even one oscillator cannot be repaired by reweighting the others.
Moreover `x_k=a_k t_k` gives `D_-k=x_k` and `D_k=c k partial_x_k`.
After adjoining a square root of c and multiplying every D by its inverse,
this is conjugate to the standard oscillator family. This is a classification
of the **operators**, not a proof that all other source identities, label
recovery, or costs hold for an arbitrary changed construction.

If c=0, all products vanish. The standard vacuum pairing generated by these
operators is degenerate on every positive grade: for occupation vectors m,n,
its entry is

    (product_k D_k^m_k D_-k^n_k 1) evaluated at t=0
      = [m=n] product_k m_k! (a_k b_k)^m_k.

Thus every entry with positive total occupation is zero. This excludes a
nondegenerate label pairing **of this vacuum-derivative form**, not other
representations or other recovery schemes. Over characteristic p, ck can
vanish at multiples of p even for c nonzero, and factorials also vanish;
the nonzero and pairing conclusions cannot be transported unchanged.

## Scope and costs

This result tests a necessary local identity, not a new contraction, solver,
or all-input lower bound. It supplies no improved alpha, no new encoding,
recursion, entry algorithm, or precision/recovery bound. Reduction branches,
queries, description sizes, finite exceptions, and space remain at the
accepted baseline because no replacement algorithm is proposed. A genuinely
new identity, non-scalar or coupled oscillators, or a different pairing is
outside the classification and is a meaningful changed premise.

## Beyond second differences: any finite scalar recurrence

A more general changed premise is a fixed finite-order scalar locality
relation. Let `(g_n)_{n>=0}` be a sequence over any field, and suppose
that for all n sufficiently large,

    p_0 g_n + p_1 g_{n-1} + ... + p_d g_{n-d} = 0,   p_0 != 0.   (2)

Here `d>=1` is a fixed upper bound on the number of lags; coefficients may
vanish. If `d` consecutive terms vanish sufficiently far out, (2) forces
all subsequent terms to vanish inductively. Consequently, if the sequence
has infinitely many nonzero terms, **every** sufficiently late block of d
consecutive indices contains a nonzero term. Its support therefore has lower
asymptotic density at least `1/d`. Equivalently, an infinite support with
arbitrarily long gaps, in particular an infinite zero-density support, is
incompatible with every such finite-order recurrence.

The density constant is sharp: `g_n=[n divisible by d]` satisfies
`g_n-g_{n-d}=0` and has density `1/d`. This is an elementary necessary
condition, independent of the actual values of nonzero terms and of field
characteristic. If a finite polynomial `P(z)` with nonzero constant term
makes `P(z) sum_n g_n z^n` a polynomial, its coefficients give (2). Thus no
such scalar polynomial locality relation can hold for an infinitely supported
weighted commutator sequence with arbitrarily long gaps. This strengthens the
predecessor's nonrational-series observation for its special `g_n=n[n in S]`
to arbitrary weights; it does not assert that any finite recurrence suffices
for the crossing identity, nor does it rule out non-scalar locality, a changed
pairing, or a recurrence order growing with the instance size.
