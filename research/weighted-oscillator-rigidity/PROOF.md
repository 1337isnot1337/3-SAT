# Rigidity of scalar-weighted oscillator repairs

This is an author-checked necessary condition for a particular modification of
Anthropic's crossing construction, not a SAT lower bound. The source and its
precise h=1 convention are pinned in `research/sparse-oscillator-locality/PROOF.md`
(base main `630041c9da3a26edb8c8f59d5f8a4132a188c8bd`). The accepted OpenAI
baseline is assumed, not audited. We credit that predecessor for the
second-difference argument and ask whether *arbitrary scalar weights* repair
its missing-mode example.

Let F be a field of characteristic zero. On F[t_1,t_2,...], let
`D_-k = a_k t_k` and `D_k = b_k partial/partial t_k` for k>0, with
`D_0=0`; any a_k or b_k may vanish. Define g(0)=0 and
`g(k)=a_k b_k` for k>0, `g(-k)=-g(k)`. Then
`[D_k,D_-k]=g(k) Id`. Retain exactly the predecessor's h=1 two-order
identity, namely

    Q_s - Q_s^rev = -(g(s-1)-2g(s)+g(s+1)) Id.

This follows by commuting the three pairs of operators with coefficients
1, -2, 1; it holds on the whole polynomial ring, including the vacuum.
It follows that this h=1 identity holds at every integer s **if and only if**

    a_k b_k = c k    for all k>=1, with c=a_1 b_1.       (1)

Indeed zero second differences force `g(k)=g(0)+k(g(1)-g(0))`
for positive and negative k; conversely this linear g has zero second
differences. The characteristic-zero assumption matters below.

If c is nonzero, (1) forces both weights nonzero at every index: deletion
of even one oscillator cannot be repaired by reweighting the others.
Moreover `x_k=a_k t_k` gives `D_-k=x_k` and `D_k=c k partial_x_k`.
After adjoining a square root of c and multiplying every D by its inverse,
this is conjugate to the standard oscillator family. This is a classification
of the **operators**, not a proof that all other source identities, label
recovery, or costs hold for an arbitrary changed construction.

If c=0, all products vanish. The standard vacuum pairing generated by these
operators is degenerate on every positive grade: for occupation vectors m,n,
its entry is

    (product_k D_k^m_k D_-k^n_k 1) evaluated at t=0
      = [m=n] product_k m_k! (a_k b_k)^m_k.

Thus every entry with positive total occupation is zero. This excludes a
nondegenerate label pairing **of this vacuum-derivative form**, not other
representations or other recovery schemes. Over characteristic p, ck can
vanish at multiples of p even for c nonzero, and factorials also vanish;
the nonzero and pairing conclusions cannot be transported unchanged.

## Scope and costs

This result tests a necessary local identity, not a new contraction, solver,
or all-input lower bound. It supplies no improved alpha, no new encoding,
recursion, entry algorithm, or precision/recovery bound. Reduction branches,
queries, description sizes, finite exceptions, and space remain at the
accepted baseline because no replacement algorithm is proposed. A genuinely
new identity, non-scalar or coupled oscillators, or a different pairing is
outside the classification and is a meaningful changed premise.

## Beyond second differences: bounded-order scalar recurrence

A more general changed premise is a fixed finite-order scalar locality
relation. Let `(g_n)_{n>=0}` be a sequence over any field, and suppose
that for all n sufficiently large,

    p_0(n) g_n + p_1(n) g_{n-1} + ... + p_d(n) g_{n-d} = 0,
    p_0(n) != 0 for every sufficiently large integer n.             (2)

Here `d>=1` is a fixed upper bound on the number of lags; the other
coefficients may vary with n and may vanish. If `d` consecutive terms vanish sufficiently far out, (2) forces
all subsequent terms to vanish inductively. Consequently, if the sequence
has infinitely many nonzero terms, **every** sufficiently late block of d
consecutive indices contains a nonzero term. Its support therefore has lower
asymptotic density at least `1/d`. Equivalently, an infinite support with
arbitrarily long gaps, in particular an infinite zero-density support, is
incompatible with every such finite-order recurrence.

The density constant is sharp: `g_n=[n divisible by d]` satisfies
`g_n-g_{n-d}=0` and has density `1/d`. This is an elementary necessary
condition, independent of the actual values of nonzero terms and of field
characteristic. If a finite polynomial `P(z)` with nonzero constant term
makes `P(z) sum_n g_n z^n` a polynomial, its coefficients give (2). Thus no
such scalar polynomial locality relation can hold for an infinitely supported
weighted commutator sequence with arbitrarily long gaps. This strengthens the
predecessor's nonrational-series observation for its special `g_n=n[n in S]`
to arbitrary weights; it does not assert that any finite recurrence suffices
for the crossing identity, nor does it rule out non-scalar locality, a changed
pairing, a leading coefficient that vanishes infinitely often, or a recurrence
order growing with the instance size. Polynomial-coefficient recurrences over
characteristic zero meet the leading-coefficient condition whenever that
polynomial is not identically zero.

## Characteristic-p control

The characteristic-zero qualification cannot be dropped. Over `F_p`, set
`a_k=1`, `b_k=k mod p` at every k. The commutator sequence is still the odd
linear sequence `g(k)=k` in the field, so all h=1 second differences vanish
and `c=1` is nonzero. Nevertheless `D_k=0` at every positive multiple of p,
so whole annihilation modes are missing. In particular `t_p` pairs to zero
with every annihilation word in the usual vacuum pairing. Also `t_1^p` pairs
to zero, because repeated differentiation introduces the zero factor p. Thus
neither the no-missing-index conclusion nor nondegeneracy of the usual Fock
pairing extends to this characteristic-p example. This says nothing about
other pairings or a complete finite-field crossing construction.

## Growth of the characteristic-p pairing quotient

Continue the preceding example over F_p, grading `t_k` by k. The vacuum
pairing between monomials is diagonal: for occupation vectors m,n it is
zero unless m=n, and then is `product_k n_k! (k mod p)^n_k`. Consequently,
the nondegenerate quotient has basis the monomials with `n_k=0` when p
divides k, and `0<=n_k<p` otherwise. Its Hilbert series is

    H_p(z) = product_{k>=1, p does not divide k} (1+z^k+...+z^((p-1)k)).

Let N_p(E) be the sum of dimensions through grade E. Uniformly over all
primes p>=2 and all sufficiently large E,

    log N_p(E) = Theta(sqrt(E)), with absolute implied constants.

For the lower bound take B=floor(sqrt(E)), and all subsets of indices
1,...,B not divisible by p, with occupancy one. Every subset has total grade
at most B(B+1)/2<=E for E>=1, and the distinct quotient basis monomials
number `2^(B-floor(B/p)) >= 2^(B/2)`, uniformly in p. For the upper bound,
this quotient's cumulative dimension is at most the number of unrestricted
partitions of all grades up to E. The standard generating-function estimate gives `exp(O(sqrt(E)))`:
for `0<t<=1`, `prod_k (1-exp(-tk))^-1 <= exp(C/t)` by expanding the
logarithm and bounding its geometric series; take t=1/sqrt(E).

Thus this natural finite-characteristic radical quotient retains square-root
*cumulative* grade growth, even when p is allowed to vary with E. The next
section strengthens this to an individual-grade bound; neither result rules
out another pairing, a new identity, or a different contraction. No resulting
SAT time is claimed.

## Uniform lower bound at every large grade

In fact, the preceding quotient has the same growth at **every sufficiently
large individual grade**, uniformly in p. Let `h_p(n)` be the coefficient of
`z^n` in `H_p(z)`. For every n>=256 choose `B=floor(sqrt(n/2))`, and take any
subset S of the indices at most B not divisible by p. Its sum is at most
`B(B+1)/2 <= n/2`, so the remaining grade R is at least n/2.

If p does not divide R, pad the monomial by the single mode R. Otherwise,
choose `a` to be B+1 unless p divides B+1, in which case take B+2. Both a
and R-a are not divisible by p. Since n>=256 implies `a<=B+2<=n/8`,
we have `R-a>a>B`; pad by these two distinct modes. In either case all
padding indices are larger than B, and all occupations are 0 or 1, strictly
less than p. Thus the small-index portion uniquely recovers S, so this is an
injection into the grade-n quotient basis. At least half the indices 1,...,B
are not divisible by p. Therefore

    h_p(n) >= 2^(B-floor(B/p)) >= 2^(B/2).

The unrestricted partition bound supplies `h_p(n)<=exp(O(sqrt(n)))` with an
absolute constant. Hence `log h_p(n)=Theta(sqrt(n))` uniformly for all
primes p and n>=256. The padding construction is part of the proof; the
finite checker independently enumerates its stated sample domain. This is a
result about the specific vacuum quotient and not a substitute for a complete
finite-field crossing construction or cost analysis.
