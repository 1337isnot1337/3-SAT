# Precision-bounded factors from rational rank alone

Status: author-checked component argument, substantially AI-assisted.
The modular matrix method is standard local-ring linear algebra. The
contribution here is its explicit crossing-factor interface and precision
ledger. No smaller crossing rank or SAT exponent is proved.

## 1. The modular factorization lemma

Let A be an integer m-by-n matrix with rational rank r, and let P>=1.
There is a deterministic algorithm, using only A modulo 2 to the power P,
which constructs U,V with

```math
 A\equiv UV\pmod {2^P},\qquad
 U\in\{0,\ldots,2^P-1\}^{m\times k},\quad
 V\in\{0,\ldots,2^P-1\}^{k\times n},\quad k\le r.
```

It takes `O(m n min(m,n))` ring operations and polynomial bit cost per
operation in P. It stores at most polynomially many matrix entries.
There is no requirement to compute small exact rational or integer factors.

Here is the algorithm used in [modular.py](modular.py). Start with residual
B=A modulo 2 to the power P. If it is zero, stop. Choose an entry B_ij of
minimum two-adic valuation v. Write B_ij=2^v w, w odd. Define

```math
 u_a=B_{aj}w^{-1}\pmod {2^P},\qquad
 v_b=B_{ib}/2^v,
```

using canonical residues for the exact bit shift in the second expression.
Replace B by B-u v. Its pivot row and column become zero. Record the outer
product u v and repeat on the remaining nonzero block. The vectors u are
divisible by 2^v, so the choice of a quotient modulo 2 to the power P-v
does not affect the outer product. Only odd w is inverted.

This step is equivalent to unit row/column operations which diagonalize
the pivot 2^v w and leave its Schur complement. Minimum valuation ensures
that the pivot divides every entry in the ring. All later pivot valuations
are at least v. Thus the final residual diagonalization is a Smith
diagonal over this chain ring, with k nonzero diagonal entries. Summing
the recorded outer products gives the displayed factorization, including
when some or all nonzero pivots are zero divisors.

For completeness, k<=r does not mean rank over F2. The integer column
lattice of A is a free abelian group of rank r; a lattice basis gives
A=BC with integer B of r columns and integer C. Heights of these exact
factors are irrelevant to this existence argument. Its image modulo
2^P is therefore generated by at most r elements. A Smith diagonal with
k nonzero ideals has image M with `dim_F2(M/2M)=k`: each nonzero ideal
`2^v Z/(2^P)` contributes exactly one dimension. Any generating set for M
must have at least k elements, proving the bound. The algorithm itself
does not compute a lattice basis or use any height bound for it.

The distinction matters: diag(2,2) modulo four needs two factors even
though its reduction over F2 has rank zero. The checker includes this
negative control and matrices whose rational rank is smaller than their
dimensions.

## 2. The crossing interface

Suppose a uniformly evaluable integer crossing family satisfies

```math
 \sum_i A_i=d_\lambda\,[x=v][y=u],\qquad d_\lambda\ne0,
```

on four lambda-bit strings, with polynomially many pieces and explicit
upper bounds r_i,c_i for the two rational flattening ranks. Assume the
normalizer and entries have polynomial bit length in lambda, and that
the pieces, ranks, normalizer and each entry can be computed in time
`2^O(lambda)`. This retains those parts of Anthropic's `def:genpieces`;
it replaces its requirement for explicit low-height integer factors.

At any requested precision P, enumerate a flattening of dimension
`2^(2 lambda)` on each side and apply Section 1. This gives two modular
factorizations with at most r_i and c_i terms, respectively. Pad their
term indices to binary codes, making invalid codes zero. Both factors
are canonical P-bit integers. Time, including all dense entry evaluations,
is `2^O(lambda) poly(P)`; space is `2^O(lambda) poly(P)`. The constants
are uniform. For r_i or c_i zero the piece vanishes and can be omitted.
The shared-bit cost is at most
`ceil(log2 r_i)+ceil(log2 c_i)`.

These factors are exact modulo 2^P. They need not be exact over Z, need
not be compatible with factors recomputed at another precision, and must
not silently be promoted to a stronger precision. Nothing here identifies
parity at a shifted interpolation point with origin parity.

## 3. Paying for a nonunit normalizer

If D is a positive integer, set v=v2(D). Knowing D Z modulo
2 to the power b+v determines Z modulo 2 to the power b: divide its
canonical residue by 2^v and invert the odd integer D/2^v modulo 2^b.
The residue is divisible by 2^v. No integer division by an odd factor of
D is needed. Negative D is handled by its sign.

For a crossing expansion with J occurrences, the multiplier is
`D=abs(d_lambda)^J` and the precision increment is exactly
`J v2(d_lambda)`. Discarding this increment is invalid: modulo eight,
the products 2*0 and 2*4 agree while 0 and 4 do not.

Consider a finite recursive tensor protocol whose only divisions are
these known crossing normalizers. Suppose every path has depth at most L,
every call has at most S wire bits and crossing occurrences, and every
lambda is at most S. If `log2(abs(d_lambda)) <= (2 lambda)^c`, then

```math
 P_* = b_0+(L+1)S(2S)^c
```

is a sufficient global precision cap to recover b_0 bits at the root.
Each child is requested at its parent's output precision plus the parent's
normalizer valuation. The sum of increments on any path is below this cap.
Branches do not add their precision requirements: each returned residue is
summed at the same parent precision.

Compute every modular factor at this SAME global cap P_*. Freeze its
canonical representative for every later oracle call, reducing it only
when a lower precision is requested. All crossing identities therefore
hold at P_* and at every precision used by the recursion. This avoids any
assumption that a factorization created at lower precision remains valid
when promoted. Tensor entry products and sums can always be reduced modulo
P_*'s power of two; they do not require unbounded integer lifts.

At a parent, the sum of child residues is congruent to D times the parent
value at the requested increased precision. The preceding division rule
then returns exactly its required residue. Induction on remaining depth
proves correctness. The full child sum need not be divisible by the odd
part of D as an integer; congruence and its unit inverse are sufficient.

## 4. Quantitative compatibility with the master recurrence

This is a transfer argument for the same tensor protocol and cost analysis,
not a new choice of crossing pieces. Suppose the rank-only family also has

```math
 \lceil\log_2r_i\rceil+\lceil\log_2c_i\rceil
 \le4\rho\lambda+c\log_2(2\lambda),\qquad 1/2\le\rho<1.
```

Use the networks, grouping, parameters and recovery order of Anthropic's
`thm:master`. Its algebraic expansions and regroupings are sums and products
over the working ring; the final division is replaced by Section 3. Its
factor-entry cost `2^O(lambda)` becomes `2^O(lambda) poly(P_*)`, and all
factor/table arithmetic uses at most P_* bits after reduction. The same
proof bounds the cost by its original exponential term times

```math
 (K(\lambda+B+P_*))^{C K^2}
```

for a larger constant C depending on the rank-only contract. The dense
matrix construction is included in the `2^O(lambda)` factor. It is not
an uncharged preprocessing step or an oracle for free.

Here is a polynomial bound for P_* in that application. Write N=K^2 lambda
for a call's wire budget. The source's displayed child-size inequality
gives

```math
 N_{\rm child}\le(\rho+3\zeta)N
       +(c/4)N\log_2(2\lambda)/\lambda+K_{\rm old}^2
       \le(c+3)N,
```

since zeta<=1/16, lambda>=1, log2(2 lambda)<=lambda and
K_old squared<=N. The depth is O(log log N_root) by its parameter
recurrence. Thus all call sizes are bounded by a fixed polynomial in
N_root, even with these deliberately loose estimates. Each crossing count
is at most its call size, so Section 3 gives a polynomial P_* when the
root output precision is polynomial in the input size.

For the final exact SAT count, n+1 bits suffice, because it lies between
zero and 2^n. Intermediate queries must use their OWN proved precision
bounds. In particular the source's `app:onelog` sums network profiles modulo
`2^beta` for its greedy check potential and modulo `2^(n+1)` for the final
count. Evaluate each profile at that sum's modulus, then recover its bounded
integer potential before the greedy comparison. This keeps every candidate
check, including rejected candidates. A final-count modulus alone must not
be substituted for beta. Those precision bounds and the outer network
sizes are polynomial in the input. Consequently
`log(P_*)=O(log(n+m))`. The additional polynomial factor in
P_* is absorbed by the same master prefactor and the same final choice
of K; no new leading exponential term appears. Reduction branches,
grouping enumeration, queries, recursion and dense-input overheads are
otherwise unchanged. No randomness is introduced.

Subject to this rank-only family and the same source construction, the
time transfer is therefore the same `alpha'=log2(1/rho)` master envelope,
with its same density correction. This is a sufficient replacement
interface, not evidence that a smaller rho exists. At the available
rho=1/sqrt(2), it yields no improvement of alpha or the selected bound.

The generic dense factorization uses exponential space in lambda. We do
NOT claim to preserve the accepted theorem's polynomial-space guarantee.
Its space is bounded by its running time, hence by the corresponding
subexponential time envelope when the full assumptions hold. A polynomial-
space implementation of this generic compiler remains a separate problem.

## 5. Scope and source boundaries

The normalizer hypothesis cannot be discarded. For example let H=2^lambda
and let B_H=2I-S, with S the nilpotent superdiagonal shift. It has tiny
integer entries, but

```math
 B_H^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{H-1}2^{-j-1}S^j
```

has top-right entry `2^-H`. Any integer multiple clearing its denominators
has two-adic valuation at least H. A generic inverse used for recovery can
therefore introduce exponential precision loss even when the original
matrix entries are small. The rank-factor compiler never needs that inverse;
it does not make such a normalization free.

The proof does not supply a smaller rank family, a filter eliminating a
central interval of expensive shifts, or uniform inverses for a singular
moment pairing. It removes the need to exhibit small exact integer factors
once a suitable **uniform low-height integer piece family with both rational
rank bounds** is actually proved. The frozen source, prior rank screens
and charged-moment work receive credit in SOURCES.json and README.md.
Standard Smith/local-ring elimination is not claimed as a new algorithm.
The transfer has not received independent or formal review, and a complete
new master/SAT implementation is not claimed by the bounded checker.
