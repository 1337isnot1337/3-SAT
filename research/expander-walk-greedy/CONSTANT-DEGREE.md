# Constant-degree refinement via Alon's explicit expanders

## External theorem and uniform construction

Alon's [arXiv:2003.11673v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11673v1), Theorem 1.3, supplies, for each fixed d>=3 and epsilon>0 and all sufficiently large admissible q, a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm outputting a d-regular q-vertex graph with all nontrivial adjacency eigenvalues bounded in absolute value by 2 sqrt(d-1)+epsilon. Use d=100 and epsilon=1; 100q is always even and 2 sqrt(99)+1 < 50. Thus the normalized absolute nontrivial eigenvalues are below 1/2. The cited theorem is an external input; this package does not implement or independently verify its expander construction. The cited v1 PDF has SHA-256 `db70b3ea82227959408cf845366b7235d4b9f611607cdebf1a1fc383cb2f498c`.

To make the finite boundary explicit without needing a numerical threshold, on input q run the fixed theorem's polynomial-time construction with its prescribed polynomial clock. Check that its output is an undirected 100-regular q-vertex multigraph and that both rational symmetric matrices I/2-(T-J/q) and I/2+(T-J/q) are positive semidefinite, where T is the normalized adjacency matrix and J is the all-ones matrix. Rational positive-semidefiniteness can be tested in polynomial bit time by exact symmetric elimination, including zero-pivot checks. If the construction fails, exceeds its clock, or either check fails, use the complete looped graph on q vertices: each vertex has one edge to every vertex, degree q, normalized adjacency J/q, and zero nontrivial eigenvalues. The output graph is always regular, explicitly enumerable, and has the required spectral bound. By the cited theorem only finitely many q trigger fallback, so the output degrees are bounded by one fixed constant D, even though this proof does not give a numerical value for D. Every output is constructible in poly(q) time and space. The finite fallback is part of the algorithm, not a claim that Alon's graph exists for small q.

This is a theorem-dependent uniform reduction, with an implicit constant and no local implementation of the external construction or exact PSD tester. It does not constitute a practical benchmark. The simple self-contained O(log m)-degree construction in PROOF.md remains available without this external theorem.

## Composition

Use q as the least power of two at least m and embed the m indices as in PROOF.md. Replace the Walsh walk by the graph above. The avoidance proof uses only symmetry, regularity and the absolute spectral bound, so the same 1/9 lower bound on hitting every support of size at least m/P holds. Use the h^2<=16P^2 coefficient strings of SMALL-BIAS.md. Each candidate is still a list of P pairs and has rejection probability at least 1/36. The same 28n greedy rounds therefore give at most 28n q D^(P-1) h^2 candidate calls and one final count, with at most 31n+1 equations per candidate. Since q<2m, this is m 2^{O(P)} poly(P,n) calls, with all discarded candidate calls charged. Constructing the graph, testing its spectrum, constructing the field and enumerating each list all take polynomial space, as do the source count calls. The multiset includes all edge-port choices and retains multiplicity.

For the source's polylogarithmic P and B, this is still exp(polylog n) overhead next to the unchanged contraction envelope. Neither a new alpha nor a full SAT implementation is asserted. External theorem dependencies, author-checked proof, and the lack of independent review remain explicit.
