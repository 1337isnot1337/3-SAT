# Constant-degree checks via a non-Cayley expander

This follow-up composes the local code in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md) with a
published expander theorem. It is an author-checked argument, not independent
review. Unlike the preceding XOR-Cayley construction, this is a non-Cayley
port graph. Explicit asymptotically good sparse-check expander codes are
classical; see [Sipser and Spielman, *Expander Codes*](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/spielman/PAPERS/expandersIT.pdf),
IEEE Transactions on Information Theory 42 (1996), 1710--1722, SHA-256
`2b2d441b6c23aceb7288c221c4f2640d0ec3d92c1b1797b4500e2ad8f7e6415f`.
We do not claim novelty for their fixed-density existence result. The scoped
contribution here is an explicit B-dependent row/distance ledger and its
interface with this repository's reduction. No SAT exponent improvement is
claimed.

## External theorem and source pin

The Margulis--Gabber--Galil graph on `(Z/tZ)^2` has eight permutation ports
and normalized nontrivial spectral radius at most `5 sqrt(2)/8 < 9/10` for
every integer t>=2. We use this theorem as a cited external result; it is not
proved by the finite checks below. The precise port construction and bound are
stated in Hoory, Linial and Wigderson, *Expander Graphs and Their
Applications*, Bulletin AMS 43 (2006), Construction 8.1 and Theorem 8.2,
[author-hosted published PDF](https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/PUBLICATIONS/MYPAPERS/HLW06/hlw06.pdf),
SHA-256 `b733e23c10bb94448e86e86cd2a7300e7469b3a8c393214e0fdd14ba90661bd4`
(captured 2026-10-28 UTC). The theorem is attributed there to O. Gabber and
Z. Galil, *Explicit constructions of linear-sized superconcentrators*,
J. Comput. System Sci. 22 (1981), 407--420,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000%2881%2990040-4); we did not inspect
the original article. The PDF is not bundled; no redistribution license is
asserted.

## Parameters and graph

Let n,m,B be positive integers with m<=Bn. Set B'=4B and

    L = ceil(log2(B'+2)),       K = 2^24 (B')^2 L,
    a = ceil(log2(2K)),         k = 6a,
    D = 8^k,                    P = 2K^2,    d = min(m,D).

Since `(5 sqrt(2)/8)^6=(25/32)^3 < 1/2`, the normalized spectral
radius raised to k is at most `1/(2K)`. Also
`D=8^(6 ceil(log2(2K))) <= (4K)^18`, so D and P are polynomial in numeric B,
independent of m. Their constants and polynomial degree are extremely large.

If m<=D, use one vertex per side and d=m parallel edges. Otherwise d=D; put
`t=ceil(sqrt(m/d))` and q=t^2. Then t>=2 and

    m <= qd < 4m.

Take the k-step walk graph of the cited eight-port graph on q vertices, and
its bipartite double cover. Each of the d=8^k ports is a word of k base
permutations. At a left vertex follow that word; at a right vertex the same
port identifies the unique inverse-word predecessor. Thus every vertex on
both sides has one incident edge of each port even with loops, parallel edges,
and repeated endpoints. The normalized nonconstant spectral radius is at
most `(5 sqrt(2)/8)^k <= 1/(2K)`; the bipartite incidence matrix therefore has
the source mixing bound with epsilon=1/(2K). This is a bound on its
nonconstant singular values, not on the double cover's trivial +/-d spectrum.

## Local code, distance, and row count

Apply the concatenated local-code lemma proved in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md), with
B' instead of B. It supplies a length-d binary code with relative distance at
least delta=1/K and at most

    c = ceil(d/(8B')) = ceil(d/(32B))

binary parity checks, constructible in time and space polynomial in d and B.
For d<K the same lemma uses zero rows and the distance assertion is still
valid. Place those c rows at each vertex of the bipartite graph and shorten
from qd to m edge coordinates as in the accepted Tanner construction. In the
one-vertex case the mixing inequality is immediate. Otherwise apply the cited
spectral theorem and the source mixing proof. Every nonzero kernel word has
weight at least

    delta(delta-epsilon) qd = qd/(2K^2) >= m/P.

Each check has at most d nonzeros. Each retained column occurs in at most
2c rows, since its edge meets exactly two vertices and each has c checks;
parallel edges retain separate port identities. When m>D, d=D>=32B and
`c<=d/(16B)`, hence `2qc<=qd/(8B)<m/(2B)<=n`. When m<=D,
`2c<=m/(16B)+2<=3n`. Thus the result has at most 3n checks, each of weight
at most D(B), and relative kernel distance at least 1/P(B).

Construction is uniform and polynomial in numeric n,m,B. The graph has qd<4m
edge coordinates; its ports are generated by k affine permutations and their
inverses, with k=O(log(B+2)). The local-code construction is polynomial in d and
B, and d<=m; enumerate all ports, assemble the local rows, and shorten in
polynomial time and space. For fixed B, D and P are constants. The result is
therefore a constant-row-weight, constant-relative-distance sparse-check
matrix with O(n) rows. The XOR-Cayley degree lower bound in PROOF.md does not
apply to this different graph.

## Near-rate-one isolation encoding

For any fixed positive integer L, the preceding construction also reduces
the sparse-isolation code length from the predecessor's 8n to

    N = n + ceil(n/L),    B = 2L,    n >= 4L.

The check construction applies because N<=2n<=Bn. If its graph has more
than one vertex on a side, its row count is less than N/(2B)=N/(4L), at most
n/(2L)<=ceil(n/L). If it has one vertex on each side, the row count is at
most N/(16B)+2 <= n/(16L)+2 <= n/L. In both cases the kernel dimension is at
least n. Its relative distance is at least 1/P(2L), a positive constant for
fixed L, and each check has at most D(2L) nonzeros.

By Gaussian elimination select a linearly independent *subset of the
original check rows*, rather than replacing them by dense linear combinations.
Let its rank be r. Select an information set of N-r kernel coordinates and
designate n of them for the input assignment; set the remaining N-r-n
information coordinates to zero. Each assignment then extends to exactly one
codeword. The resulting representation uses N-n=ceil(n/L) auxiliary bits and
exactly r+(N-r-n)=N-n equations: r selected constant-weight checks and
N-r-n unary zero checks. Each encoded variable appears in at most 2c+1 of
these code equations. Distinct encoded assignments remain at relative
distance at least 1/P(2L). The information-set computation and row selection
are polynomial time and space. Handle n<4L by direct enumeration; for fixed L
this is a finite exception.

The predecessor's isolation argument therefore still applies with
`O_L(log n)` sampled coordinates per isolation row. Its subsequent queries,
error budget, sparse hashing, tensor encoding and contraction remain charged.
For each fixed L this is a near-rate-one encoding with constants depending on
L; allowing L to grow with n requires a new cost analysis. The L dependence is necessary in principle: puncturing any d_min-1
coordinates of a binary length-N dimension-n linear code is injective, so
`d_min<=N-n+1=ceil(n/L)+1` (the Singleton bound). Thus a guaranteed relative
distance 1/P must satisfy `P>=N/(ceil(n/L)+1)`. In particular, if L grows
while n/L grows, this lower bound on P grows on the order of L; a fixed
positive relative distance cannot be carried through such a limit. The Hamming
packing bound sharpens this: put r=N-n and t=floor((d_min-1)/2). Disjoint
radius-t balls give `sum_{i=0}^t binom(N,i) <= 2^r`. For t>0,
`binom(N,t)>=(N/t)^t`, and t<=N/2. Hence t<=r and then

    t <= r / log2(N/r),
    d_min/N <= 2r/(N log2(N/r)) + 2/N.

In particular P=Omega(L log L) when L and n/(L log L) both tend to infinity.
These are coding bounds, not SAT lower bounds. We do not claim a smaller SAT exponent
or an improved hidden leading constant in the accepted bound.

## Linear-size hashed fingerprint network at fixed density

There is a second conditional composition with the merged
[hash-syndrome-chain package](../hash-syndrome-chain/README.md). Fix B and
fixed clause width k, and consider one nontrivial leaf with m<=Bn clauses.
Use the checks above and append the predecessor's t=O(n) random violation
hash rows, each with at most 2P(B) sampled occurrences. The check matrix has
`a=O(n)` rows and at most

    h <= 3n D(B) + 2P(B)t = O_B(n)

nonzero entries. The predecessor's Boolean row network consequently has
`s=n+km+a+h=O_{B,k}(n)` wires. Its equality-tree conversion and grouped
field-weight tensors retain its stated degree, entry-oracle, bipartite,
truncated-ring and polynomial-space conditions. For field size at least 6n,
the cited grouped-weight construction evaluates the hashed fingerprint in
`poly(input) * 2^{O_{B,k}(n/sqrt(log n))}` time and polynomial space. Build
the checks and random rows in polynomial time and use the predecessor's
finite-exception fallback. This is one hashed-fingerprint evaluation for a
fixed-density leaf, not a new unrestricted or fixed-density SAT bound: the
accepted exact counter already has the same fixed-density exponent. For
B growing with n, D(B) and P(B) are not constants and this linear-size claim
must not be used.

## A limitation of the inherited isolation sampler

The isolation wrapper samples J coordinates independently with uniform
coefficient bits and requires each pairwise collision bias to be at most
`eta=(2n)^(-4)`. For this particular sampling rule, J=Omega(log n) is
necessary even if the code has constant relative distance. Indeed, any
binary linear code of dimension r>=2 on N coordinates has a nonzero word of
weight at most 2N/3: at each nonzero coordinate exactly 2^(r-1) of its 2^r
codewords have a one, so averaging over the nonzero words gives that bound.
Apply this to the dimension-n subcode of encoded assignments, for n>=2.

Let w be the weight of that nonzero difference and R>=N the padded size.
One sampled occurrence with an independent uniform coefficient has parity
sign expectation `1-w/R`; J independent occurrences give collision bias
exactly `(1-w/R)^J >= 3^(-J)`, even when repeated coordinates cancel.
Thus the stated universal pairwise-bias requirement forces

    J >= 4 log(2n)/log 3.

Our fixed-B substitution has P constant, so the predecessor's J=O(P log n)
sampling length matches this lower bound in order. This is a bound on the
number of sampled occurrences for this particular independent sampler and its
pairwise-bias target. It is not a lower bound on the final row support, on
other hash distributions, or on isolation or SAT algorithms generally.

## SAT composition and evidence boundary

At B=8, substituting these checks into the existing sparse-code isolation
interface gives constant-weight code checks and isolation rows with O(log n)
terms. The full accepted contraction still incurs its stated general-density
exponent; the fixed-density counter and selected alpha remain unchanged. For
polylogarithmic B the very high-degree polynomial D(B) is still polylogarithmic,
but may be much worse than the earlier constructions; no practical gain is
asserted. As a separate cost-model observation,
[PR #114](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/3-SAT/pull/114) studies an
explicit positive-coefficient sparsification/width proxy. Its c=0 case still
has a log(lambda)/sqrt(lambda) optimized term when a positive multiple of
log B is charged. This supports the stated limitation only within that
proxy; it is not a lower bound on actual leaf density or SAT runtime.

`verify_expander.py` checks the eight port permutations, symmetry, and exact
forward/inverse powered-port identities, rational mixing inequalities for every pair of vertex subsets on sides t=2,3
and powers 1,2,3; exact rational PSD checks of the weaker 9/10 spectral
bound for sides t=2,...,6; and 700 integer padding/row-budget schedules. It also
checks the two-dimensional subcode averaging bound, small exact sampler
biases, finite near-rate row-count inequalities, and all binary check-row
families on up to three coordinates for the information-set construction,
plus complete full and shortened kernels of a scaled 32-edge
Tanner fixture using the tested inner checks. That fixture does not meet the
large source parameters. The verifier does not
prove the cited all-size spectral theorem, construct its huge required power,
run a source-scale Tanner instance, or independently review the concatenated
local code or SAT composition. Those remain explicit limitations.
